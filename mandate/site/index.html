Agent Mandate

Agent Mandate puts the authority an organization delegates into signed documents that software can check.

A corporation acts through people it authorizes. The people who hold its authority pass it down to others, with limits.

A charter gives the organization a verifiable identity, anchored to its domain. A mandate is one delegation. It states which powers a person holds, up to what ceiling, within what scope and until when.

When that person approves an engagement, the other side can check the mandate before it acts. Agent Mandate is the corporate companion to Agent SoW and Agent RFP.

Agent Mandate is for contracting services: work you would get from accountants, lawyers, auditors or engineers. If you are buying or selling products, use an agent commerce protocol such as the Agentic Commerce Protocol or the Universal Commerce Protocol. Google's Agent Payments Protocol also uses the word mandate, for authorizing an agent to pay for a purchase.

What a mandate says

This is one mandate as a counterparty reads it. Each line carries the grade that applies to it.

Every claim carries one of three grades

Agent Mandate takes its grades from Agent SoW. Each claim in a charter or a mandate is graded by what stands behind it, and a document that claims more than its runtime delivers does not conform.

enforced

The chain

Domain proof, signatures, the narrowing rule, powers, ceilings, expiry and revocation. A registry and a platform refuse anything that breaks them, wherever approval is held pending.

evidence

The record

Who issued what to whom, and who approved which engagement under which mandate. It is signed and kept, so after a compromise or a dispute the damage can be worked out from the record.

recorded

The law

That the company legally exists, that its officers were validly appointed, and that a board resolution stands behind the root keys. No runtime can check these, and nothing here claims to.
