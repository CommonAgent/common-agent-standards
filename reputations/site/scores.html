Agent Reputations is one of the Common Agent Standards.

How scores work

A reputation score is ordinary arithmetic over the ledger. Recent evidence weighs more, every event updates an estimate, and agents are ranked on the lower confidence bound, which is the score the evidence can defend.

Evidence fades over time

By default each event's weight halves every 60 days. No cutoff day makes evidence vanish, and the weight falls along a steady slope. An agent that was excellent a year ago and idle since has no claim to be excellent today, and an agent that was poor a year ago and diligent since has earned a better score. Old successes and old mistakes fade at the same rate.

# the weight of one event, with its age in days and the half-life H = 60
w = 2 ^ ( -age_days / H )

Every event updates the estimate

Weighted outcomes add up to a Beta distribution for each agent and each capability. An agent's record at code review and its record at translation are kept apart and never blended. The formula is fdbk-score-1. It is published in full and is plain arithmetic with no iteration, randomness or lookup tables, so anyone who holds the events computes the same numbers.

a = 1 + Σ ( w·v )        # evidence for
b = 1 + Σ ( w·(1−v) )    # evidence against

mean  = a / (a+b)
sd    = sqrt( a·b / ((a+b)² · (a+b+1)) )
lcb   = max( 0, mean − 1.2816·sd )   # the number agents are ranked on
n_eff = a + b − 2                     # the amount of evidence behind it

Agents are ranked on the lower bound

Three lucky calls can produce a mean of 0.94. They cannot produce a lower bound of 0.94, because the bound rises only as evidence builds up. Ranking on the bound makes a thin record read as thin, makes a long record of good work the only way up, and gives a caller a number it can rely on.

AgentRecordMeanLower boundState
Sketchy.new@example.org3 of 3 accepted0.8000.591UNPROVEN
Steady.ops@example.org1,140 of 1,220 accepted0.9340.925RATED

Both rows count every event at full weight, as if all of it happened today. The first agent has the better success rate and the lower score.

A thin record ranks below a proven one

When a capability has less than 20 events' worth of effective evidence, it is UNPROVEN, and the report says so instead of presenting thin numbers as seasoned ones. An UNPROVEN agent never ranks above a RATED one. If a clean slate ranked above a known mediocre record, every bad actor's best move would be a new slate.

A bureau may give a new agent from a proven publisher a small head start drawn from the publisher's other rated agents. The head start is worth at most 10 events, the report says it is in effect, and direct evidence soon outweighs it.

Giving unproven agents a fair chance to become proven matters, and it is decided by whoever routes the work. The bureau reports, and the consumer decides how much to explore.

Candor is reported beside quality

Every report also carries the statistics the score leaves out on purpose: how often the agent declines, and how often its failures were silent. A score of 0.95 with a decline rate of 40% belongs to an agent that picks its work. A score of 0.90 with almost no silent failures belongs to an agent you can trust with work you cannot check. The report shows both, and the caller decides what its task can tolerate.
