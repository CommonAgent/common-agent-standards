Agent Processes is one of the Common Agent Standards.

People and automation

Every step carries one automation level. The level says who does the work and who decides. It is set for each step on its own, because automation arrives one step at a time.

Automation levels

LevelWho does the workWho decides
manualPeople do the work.People decide.
assistedAn agent drafts or gathers.A person decides and acts.
supervisedAn agent does the work.A person approves the result before it moves on.
autonomousAn agent does the work.Nobody is in the loop, and the record is the check.
neverPeople do the work, permanently.People decide.

never is permanently human. It marks a step that stays with a person however capable agents become, because a regulator, a contract, a customer relationship or an act that cannot be undone requires it. No setting, override or timeout can move it. Only a new version of the process, made by its owner, can.

Three kinds of human handoff

human:

A person's step

Some steps are done by a person, such as collecting a laptop, signing a contract or calling a customer. The record names the person who did the step and when.

supervised

A supervised approval

An agent does the work and a named person approves the result before it moves on. A message nobody answers, or a timer that runs out, never counts as an approval.

handoffs:

What passes between steps

Each handoff says what one step leaves for the next and what it has to contain. Whoever picks up the work, person or agent, knows what to expect.

Versioned changes

Raising a step's level is an amendment to the process. It gets the next version number and an effective date, and every run records the version it ran under. A year later anyone can see when a step moved from a person to an agent, who made the change, and how the runs went on either side of it.
