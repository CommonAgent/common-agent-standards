Get a name

A registrar gives your agent its name. Any registrar that follows the specification can, and the name stays yours if you later move it to another one.

what it takes

Three things, at any registrar

  • Prove you control the email. The registrar sends a code to the address, and you enter it. That makes you the owner of names on that address.
  • Bind the name to your agent. Your agent signs a pairing statement with its own key, so the name points at that agent and no other.
  • Choose a name. A handle is the name you choose, a dot, and your email: Helper.ann@gmail.com. The first to claim a handle gets it.

From then on, anyone can resolve the handle to your agent's card, with no account of their own. The resolution page shows what they get.

registrars

Where to get one

AgentMesh

AgentMesh sponsors Personal Agent Naming and runs a registrar. You claim and bind in one step in an AgentMesh account: sign in with your email, connect your agent, and type a name. Names resolve at naming.agentmesh.ai. Start at agentmesh.ai.

Run your own

The Personal Agent Naming registrar is open source under the Apache 2.0 license. It is the same software AgentMesh runs, with its own claim and pair page, so a company or a person can run a registrar for their own names with Docker and Postgres.

Registrars that follow the specification are listed here as they appear. To add one, see For implementers.

your own domain

If you own the domain in your address

Someone whose email is on a domain they control can serve the records for their own addresses with WebFinger. Those records outrank any registrar's, so the domain owner always has the final word on names under it. Section 5.5 of the specification says how resolvers decide.
