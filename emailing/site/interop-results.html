Interop results

A quote request across email providers

On 29 October 2026, from 13:06 to 13:09 Mountain time, agents on AgentMail, Primitive and Resend exchanged a quote request through the Agent Emailing Server. Every lane that could run passed every check, including a forged quote that was refused. Two lanes could not run, because the Resend key used can only send.

The results below are taken from the run's own files: interop-2026-10-29.md, interop-2026-10-29.json and the script that ran it, tools/interop.mjs (npm run interop).

What was tested

The test

A buyer's purchasing agent at example.org asks a supplier's sales agent at example.com for a quote, in four emails in one thread:

  1. Request: the request, with a parts list (CSV) and a limit of 500.00 USD.
  2. Needs your input: a question about a split delivery.
  3. Answer: the buyer's answer.
  4. Done: the quote (PDF), the price and the delivery dates.

A lane “X → Y” puts the buyer on provider X and the supplier on provider Y, so emails 1 and 3 travel from X to Y, and emails 2 and 4 from Y to X. Each lane also runs the carrier test of section 12.3 from X to Y, and sends a forged quote from Y to X: email 4 with its price raised after it was signed. The buyer's server must refuse the forgery, and the check a mail program runs must show it as a failure.

Every email is checked for delivery and time, signature, name card, text and subject, files, thread and task state, with the custom headers that arrived and the authentication results the receiving provider reports.

What could not run

AgentMail → Resend and Primitive → Resend were not run. The Resend key used can only send, so a supplier on Resend could not receive the request.

The Resend party

The Resend party sends through Resend and receives in a Google Workspace mailbox read through the Gmail API, because the Resend key can only send. So in the Resend → AgentMail and Resend → Primitive lanes, emails 2 and 4 arrived in that mailbox, not at Resend.

What the providers kept and reported

Mail sent through Primitive arrived with only the Auto-Submitted header, because Primitive accepts only three custom headers on outbound mail. Mail sent through AgentMail and Resend arrived with all of its custom headers. Nothing an agent acts on depends on them: everything is in the signed envelope.

SPF and DMARC passed on every email. DKIM was aligned for agentmail.to, primitive.email and agentmesh.ai. When AgentMail received mail sent through Resend, it reported a DKIM pass without naming the domain.

Fixes found on the way

The run found two faults in the server, both fixed before the results above (the commit says so):

  • The AgentMail poller now looks back, because a message's timestamp is its sender's Date in whole seconds, and it remembers what it has handed over.
  • Idempotency keys now cover the sender, the recipients and the subject, so the same envelope sent to another address is not taken as a retry.
